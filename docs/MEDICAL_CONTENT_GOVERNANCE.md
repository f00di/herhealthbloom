# Medical Content Governance

## Content Authority

The named owner/author is Dr. Farkhanda Kashif, FCPS, MRCOG, Obstetrician and Gynecologist. The application must not claim an external review, affiliation, certification, award, or accreditation that is not documented.

## Evidence and Citation Standards

Published claims should retain the authoritative source document's meaning and references. New claims require appropriate current medical literature, established clinical guidelines, or peer-reviewed research selected and approved by the medical editor. Developers must never fabricate citations, statistics, dates, or reviewers.

## Content Update Policy

Every article can store optional published/updated dates, but unknown dates are omitted. The review interval is to be confirmed by the site owner/medical editor. A date must represent a real editorial event.

## Emergency Information

Approved urgent-care advice uses the reusable prominent emergency component, appears early enough to be discoverable, remains visible without JavaScript, and is not softened or visually buried. Generic site copy directs a reader who believes they face an emergency to an appropriate emergency healthcare service without assuming a country or number.

## Disclaimer Rules

The site consistently states that it provides educational and informational women's health content and does not replace professional medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment. Disclaimers appear in the footer, article endings, legal page, About, and contextually on Contact without interrupting every paragraph.

## Content Editing Rules

Developers may correct obvious typography, punctuation, heading semantics, and layout while preserving meaning. They must not automatically alter diagnosis claims, treatment or medication recommendations, pregnancy emergency advice, thresholds, quantities, reference claims, clinical statistics, author credentials, or review dates. Suspected issues are documented for authorized medical review.

## Editorial Workflow

```text
Draft → Medical Review → Approved → Published → Periodic Review
```

Repository status or a successful build is not evidence of medical approval. Pull requests should identify the source version, reviewer decision, changed clinical passages, references, and real review date.

## Current Medical-Review Notes

The following source-derived wording was intentionally retained and should be reviewed by the authorized medical editor rather than silently changed by a developer:

- Pregnancy nutrition quantities and limits, including caffeine below 200 mg/day and no more than two portions of oily fish per week, may depend on the intended jurisdiction and current guidance.
- The statement that vitamin B12 is found “exclusively in animal-derived foods” may need clarification for fortified foods or supplements.
- Statements about possible vitamin D, iodine, choline, iron, B12, zinc, and DHA supplementation require individualized professional context; the article consistently directs readers to a healthcare provider.
- Pregnancy emergency, medication, imaging, antibiotic, laxative, and supportive-care wording in the abdominal-pain article must be signed off as supplied before launch.
- Reference access dates and the 2025–2030 Dietary Guidelines citation were preserved from the supplied article. URL destinations were checked during implementation, but link availability is not medical approval.

No clinical date, reviewer identity, or claim of completed external review was added.
